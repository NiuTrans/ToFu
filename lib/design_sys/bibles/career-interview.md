# Career Interview & Project Retrospective Bible

> Job interviews, project walkthroughs and work portfolios. Help the audience
> verify what the candidate did, why it mattered, and how it fits the role.

## Contribution and evidence first

- Use supplied work records as the authority for personal claims. Never invent
  employment, seniority, team size, ownership, revenue or performance gains.
- Distinguish **I implemented / I decided / I collaborated** from team results.
  A team outcome is not proof that the candidate personally caused it.
- For each result retain the baseline, time window, unit and measurement method.
  If a metric is unavailable, describe the supported qualitative result; never
  substitute a made-up percentage or proficiency score.
- Separate measured outcomes, estimates and future proposals. Remove identifying
  client details when the user asks for anonymization; do not invent substitutes.

## Story the interviewer can follow

Open with the target role and strongest relevant evidence. For each project:
problem and constraints → personal responsibility → decision and alternatives
→ action → observed result → lesson and what would change next time.
Prefer two or three deep examples to a wall of project names or technology logos.
Keep technical depth in backup pages; the main sequence must fit the speaking time.
Conclude with supported role fit, not generic enthusiasm or a bare thank-you page.

## Visual grammar

Show a readable system diagram, actual work artifact, before/after comparison or
timeline. Label conceptual diagrams as such. Synthetic dashboards, generated
screenshots and decorative charts must never impersonate work evidence.
Use one conclusion per page, clear role/result labels and consistent margins.
Keep text, diagrams and data charts editable. Preserve supplied brand/template
requirements and provide a small source/record caption where appropriate.

## Interview preparation

Speaker notes can hold likely questions about tradeoffs, failure modes, causality,
collaboration and limitations. Notes explain the visible evidence; they must not
add accomplishments or numbers absent from the supplied records.
